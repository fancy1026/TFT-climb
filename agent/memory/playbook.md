# Playbook: the game model I decide with

Generated by agent/model/build_memory.py from agent/model/game-model.md (active principles only). Each planning state also carries the CALCULATOR lines: the arithmetic of that moment (interest steps, when a level arrives and what it costs, where to roll for the units still needed, odds, what a loss costs, the HP runway).

Each section says how the game works, then **What follows**: the general principles that follow from it, learned
from strong players' games, MetaTFT and our own games. A principle sits under the mechanism it follows from; one that
cannot be placed under a mechanism is not knowledge. Levels, stages, comps and numbers in a principle are examples of
it, never the rule. Numbers are computed by the calculator (CALCULATOR lines in every planning state), never
memorised. Each principle has an id, a status and a scope: [R<n> active general] or [R<n> active patch]; only active
principles are read by the player, candidates wait for the replay test (agent/model/replay.py). When a principle
decides a choice, name its id in the reasons. The evidence behind each principle is in agent/model/evidence.md.
## 1. The goal
Be eliminated later than the other seven. Everyone starts at 100 HP and is out at 0. Placement is decided by HP over
time, so every resource matters only as the strength it buys before you would be eliminated. Gold left at the end is
worth nothing.

What follows:
- [R1 active general] Gold, interest and delayed rewards are worth only the rounds you will still play. When few rounds remain -
  late in the game, or at low HP at any stage - turn the bank into levels and rolls now, spend before the loss rather
  than after it, and take a smaller reward now over a larger one later. A 10-gold interest step pays 1 a round; over
  two or three rounds that is little against one lost fight. Hold a reserve only while the board wins clearly.

## 2. How HP is lost
HP lost in a round = P(lose the fight) x damage if lost.
- Damage if lost = stage damage + 1 per enemy unit still alive. Stage damage rises every stage, so a loss late costs
  more than a loss early, and a close loss costs less than a blowout. Winning costs nothing.
- Because early losses are cheap, early HP is a budget: it can be spent to build resources for later.
- P(lose) is about this opponent's board against yours, at this moment. Strength is relative: to the board you meet
  and, for placement, to the other seven's HP. What the lobby is building, and when it will spike, is part of the
  decision.

What follows:
- [R2 active general] Spend the HP budget only while it can pay for the wait. The CALCULATOR's HP runway shows what is left at
  the planned spike if fights keep being lost; when it cannot pay, bring the spike forward - buy the level now and roll
  there - rather than keep banking or spend at odds that do not fit (section 5).
- [R3 active general] Spend for strength now when the board can win now: a streak worth protecting, a weak lobby, a level that
  wins the next fights. The streak's gold and the HP it saves can outweigh the interest given up.
- [R12 active general] A PvE round costs no HP to a board that clears it, so strength bought for it fights for
  nothing: hold rolls and buys for strength through it and spend in the phase before the next player fight, with one
  more round of interest and the loot known. An XP buy that completes a level may be made there. Stops when the board
  may lose the PvE round, or the shop shows a unit that completes a 2-star or a breakpoint now (buy it or lock the
  shop).

## 3. Why fights are won
A fight is won when your team kills their effective HP before they kill yours.
- Damage: single-target damage per second, area damage times the targets it hits, how often spells cast (mana).
- Survival: the frontline's effective HP (health, armor and magic resist, shields, healing) has to outlast the enemy's
  damage long enough for your damage to work. Tank and damage balance is this equation, not a separate rule.
- Control: crowd control, and who targets whom first.
- Positioning: decides targeting, how many units an area spell hits, and who dies first; it can be adapted to the
  opponent.
A board on paper is not a fight: the same units win or lose depending on these four.

## 4. Where a board's power comes from
- Units: how many (the level sets the slots), their cost tier (higher costs are generally stronger), their star level
  (1 to 2 to 3, more where the set allows).
- Items: completed items on the units that use them.
- Traits: breakpoints pay in steps and apply to every unit with the trait.
- Augments, Wisps and the set's own mechanic.

What follows:
- [R4 active general] A slot is worth most when it completes a breakpoint of the core trait, because the step pays every unit
  with the trait: fill toward the core trait's next step before side traits or a 1-star off-trait unit, and a level is
  worth most when its slot completes one. A unit or emblem that activates no breakpoint now is not power now. Stops
  when the core step cannot be reached with units on hand, or a side trait carries the fight.
- [R5 active general] Items are power only when completed on a fielded unit: two components that make an item become it that
  phase on the best fielded holder, rather than wait for a carry not owned yet. A lone component on a unit is half an
  item.
- [R6 active general] Units on the bench fight for no one: a unit bought for the board is fielded the same phase.
- [R7 active general] An augment is worth what it gives plus what it adds to the rest of the board and plan: held augments,
  emblems, items, owned units, levels. Pairs multiply (a level payoff with gold or XP; an emblem payoff with emblem
  cards); a trait card is worth most for the trait whose copies you already own; an economy card pays only while the
  board holds, because it is paid for in the fights lost while the bank grows.
- [R8 active patch] Coven (this set's mechanic): Essence comes from losses and its payouts grow faster than the points, so it
  pays when its line is decided once - win-streak, or lose-streak to a large cash-out - and fielded early. A half line
  (losses fed to it, cashed small) pays the HP and returns little. Read the card's value; do not guess it. Cash at the
  first step when HP cannot pay more (section 1).

## 5. The resources that buy power
- Gold buys units, rolls (what a roll can show depends on the level's shop odds and the pool), XP and Wisps.
- XP completes levels: one more slot and better odds for higher-cost units. XP pays nothing until a level completes.
- Components become items (PvE loot, carousels, augments).
- Income = base + interest (every full 10 held, up to 5) + streak gold + win gold + PvE loot + selling.
- HP is a resource too: it is spent by losing, and it buys time.
- Options are a resource: held gold, pairs and components keep choices open; spending commits them.

What follows:
- [R9 active general] Roll where your comp's missing units show most for the gold: at the level whose shop odds favour the cost
  of the units the board still needs (the CALCULATOR's "Rolling for" line gives it per level), and level and roll there
  in the same phase; use free rerolls after the level. Until then gold goes to the level and to interest, not to rolls
  at odds that rarely show those units. Examples, not rules: 4-cost carries at level 8, 3-cost carries at 7, 1-2-cost
  rerolls at their low level.
- [R10 active general] Gold that cannot reach the next interest step costs nothing to spend: spending it now - XP that brings a
  level a round earlier, a unit - buys power at no interest cost. Early in the game, when the next step is far, XP for
  one more slot wins the cheap early fights.
- [R11 active general] Gold above the interest cap earns nothing, and holding it costs nothing: it is options. Spend it when it buys
  something now (XP toward the level that fits, the roll at that level); a partial XP buy that completes no level buys
  nothing yet.

## 6. Every decision is a trade
Each planning phase converts resources: power now (fewer HP lost now) against resources for later (interest, a level,
gold for a roll, a streak), and one kind of resource against another.

Take an action when its expected value is positive:
  EV = expected benefit - cost - opportunity cost (what the gold, HP or option would otherwise have done)
A Wisp that costs 2 and returns 4 is worth buying; whether the 2 gold costs interest, a level this round or a roll
is part of its cost. When positive-EV actions compete for the same gold, take the set with the highest total. Price
everything in one unit: gold equivalents for resources, HP saved (and through it, placement) for fights. At low HP a
risky action is worth less than its average, because losing it ends the game.

## 7. What counts as knowledge
- Arithmetic is not knowledge to learn: interest steps, when a level arrives, streak gold, roll odds, loot are computed
  at the moment of the decision.
- A principle sits under the mechanism it follows from, says when it stops holding, and is written as the principle,
  with any level, comp or number only as an example. If it cannot be placed under a mechanism, it is a preference or
  noise.
- A habit with no benefit is not a principle, even when strong players show it.
- One game is an example. A principle needs several strong players doing it for a stated reason, or the meta data
  confirming it, and it has to predict what they do in games it was not learned from.
- Strong players' choices are strong evidence: when they differ from what the numbers suggest, assume first that they
  value something the numbers leave out (HP, the streak, the lobby, an option) and find out what.
