FIRST RULES for every comp - where anything else (a comp's notes, a stage strategy) says otherwise, these hold:
- Levelling: when the XP still needed for the next level is an exact number of XP buys (4 XP each, none left over) and you mean to take that level this round or the next, buy it now. When it is not exact, or it takes the gold below an interest step, the level waits for the next round (its passive XP) - unless this fight needs the slot now. The executor buys the next level at the start of a planning phase when its XP leaves 50 gold or more (no interest lost), up to the comp's roll level - an XP order written before that is not bought twice.
- No early level 6 while interest is not full: bank toward 50 gold first.
- Sell champions outside the comp early: the bench never fills with off-comp units. Champions from the monster rounds' loot that are not in the comp are sold the moment they arrive.
- Never leave single components on different units when two of them make an item: put them together on one fielded unit - the strongest one that holds either (items concentrate) - with the Item Remover if needed.
- From stage 4 on, completed items do not wait on the item bench: every planning phase, put them onto fielded units with a free slot. A unit with three items takes no more.
- Components: until the 4-7 monster round a single component may wait for the partner of the item you want - the 4-4 carousel and the 4-7 monster round still bring components, so items are not forced from 4-1. After the 4-7 monster round, never hold two or more single components: whatever components you have, combine them into an item on a fielded unit with a free slot, even an item outside the plan.
- Lesser Champion Duplicator: use it early, and on a copy toward a 3-star rather than to make a 2-star.
- In stage 5 a Wisp is the first priority. Wisps in stages 4 and 5: a Wisp is essential - about 70% of the focus in that stretch. Roll for one when the shop shows none, unless you are sure to win without it; you do not know the opponents' strength, so get one.
- Elder Dragon is fielded the moment he is bought, never left on the bench, even at 1 star: decide his hex before he arrives.
- Augments: use the rerolls - at 3-2 in game 65 no reroll was used.
- No XP once combat has started when it takes the gold below 50: it loses interest (game 65: level 7 bought in the 3-2 combat lost 4-5 gold of interest). Gold above 50 earns no interest, so XP that leaves 50 gold or more is bought in the fight too, and the level taken then (the executor does it). A level up too late for this fight that would go below 50 is done at the start of the next planning phase.
- Never roll during stage 2 - game 66 rolled its free rerolls there (2-1, 2-2, 2-6, 2-7): the goal is to build economy, whatever the pivot.
- Interest: an off-comp unit, fielded or on the bench, is sold in the round where its gold keeps you at or brings you to 10 - in game 66, after its 2-3 buys, selling the fielded Elise would have kept 10 gold; she was sold at 2-5 instead and 1 gold of interest was lost.
- Do not field a unit that gives no trait: at 3-3 in game 66 Alistar was fielded for no reason (no traits, 1 interest wasted); the right play was to field another Scuttlecrab.
- Coven: more Coven does not make the board stronger when you are almost dead. Plan when to take the Coven rewards (convert the Essence to loot), and once taken, field stronger champions and traits in place of the Coven units (game 71 ended at 29 HP with Camille fielded). While the game is still pivoting (the Coven rewards not taken yet), field as many Coven units as the board takes - more Coven, more rewards; a Coven unit is sold only after the cash-out, once the board plays to win at level 8.
- Damage items (AP or AD) go on damage dealers, never on a tank; a tank gets tank items. Fiddlesticks is a tank (melee, Defender), not a damage dealer: in game 71 Jeweled Gauntlet and Striker's Flail were put on him. Before giving an item, check the holder's job: tank or damage dealer.
- When you are not on a win streak and the board is not strong, spend no gold on levelling or rerolls: hold it for interest (game 71 stage 2 levelled at 2-2 and 2-5 on a loss streak and stayed at 0-10 gold) - but not when HP is already low on a losing streak and you decide to take a path of the comp's FIRST RULES that levels and rolls for its power step then.
- Items: every comp has an item plan (its FIRST RULES name its main tank, its DPS and their items). By the start of stage 5 a game has about 6 completed items - more when augments, the encounter or events give more: 3 go on the main tank and 3 on the DPS. The order is a priority, not a fixed rule: when the components can make the next item of either, the DPS's comes first, then the tank's, then the DPS's again (the DPS's 1st, the tank's 1st, the DPS's 2nd, the tank's 2nd, the DPS's 3rd, the tank's 3rd); an item the components on hand make for only one of them is built now, not held for the other's turn. Build toward that plan from the first components.
- When a loss ends the game (1 HP, or less than a loss's damage): first the XP when the gold on hand completes the next level (a slot and the next level's odds), then spend all the gold rolling for the comp's upgrades: no gold floor, sell what the board does not need (the executor does both).
- Items and the main tank (the executor does them - the state's ITEM PLAN): the main tank's three items cover the frontline's stats, one each - armor and magic resist (Gargoyle Stoneplate, Evenshroud, Bramble Vest, Dragon's Claw), health (Warmog's Armor, Sunfire Cape, Spirit Visage, Protector's Vow, Crownguard), durability (Steadfast Heart); Crownguard is a tank item, not an AP item; an item whose effect does not stack (Sunder, Shred, Burn, Wound: Evenshroud, Last Whisper, Sunfire Cape, Morellonomicon) is built once a team; the main tank stands at the front row's end on the main carry's side, so the enemies meet him before the carry.
- Concentrate the items: each item's stats multiply with the ones already on that champion, so items stacked on one holder are stronger than the same items spread over several units. Items and components go onto the strongest unit the board keeps (a 2-star before a 1-star); an Item Remover moves them there from a weaker holder, and one holder's items are never split over two units. A holder the board can do without - another unit gives its trait - is sold: its items come back to the item bench for the strongest unit, and its gold counts toward the next interest step.
- A roll that depends on a Wisp (a Wisp that changes the shops) runs only after that Wisp is bought; when the Wisp is not bought, the roll does not run (the executor drops it). Below the comp's roll level no reroll runs, so a Wisp whose value is in rolling is not bought there.
- Items go on the strongest and most suitable champion of their position (the carry's items on a fighter or carry, the tank's on a tank; the executor's item plan does it, with no fixed stand-in): suitable - the position's own kind, by MetaTFT's item data; strongest - more stars first, then the higher cost (all 1-star: 5-cost > 4-cost > ... > 1-cost; a 2-star 4-cost beats a 1-star 5-cost). The same order says which unit leaves the board for another: the weakest one goes, never a 3-star for a 2-star of a lower or equal cost.
- Sell a unit the moment you decide to: put it in the first plan's sell list - sells run first, in the fight too; a unit outside the comp sold one round late costs its interest.
- Rolls never stop to ask about a Wisp: set strategy.wisp_flag (combat and economy: buy or skip, or Wisp names) before a roll - a Wisp the flag does not cover is passed. From stage 4 the default is combat: buy.
- When the comp's board is finished, or in stage 6, and the shop shows no Wisp, the first action of the planning phase is one reroll, for a Wisp (the executor does it) - not ten seconds of other steps first.
- Evenshroud is the main tank's first item whenever its components are there (this game has armor penetration: its Sunder makes the team's damage count), whatever stat his other items cover - one a team.
